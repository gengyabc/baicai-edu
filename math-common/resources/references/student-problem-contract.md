# Student-Authored Problem Contract

Shared by both primary math bands. The band's supplement names its internal check and local problem-type, multi-part grading, and invented-quantity rules; the leaf owns execution and return signals. This contract selects no band, method, grade level, activity, or board action. "Grade" means marking the AI's answer.

## The Student Decides the Problem

Invite one decision about the current relationship: a scene, numbers, unknown, or condition. The student composes; a hint names only the current point, not a complete problem to endorse. Everyday scenes are welcome, not a required template. Grade level adjusts language, not permitted problem types. Authoring may be declined without evidence of weakness.

Check conditions, units, and what is asked:

| The submitted problem | Action |
|---|---|
| Complete and valid | Answer it directly; the student's problem takes priority over a template |
| Beyond the current focus | Confirm whether to continue or return to the original goal |
| Overly large numbers | May invite the student to shrink them |
| Method not yet taught | Not a validity flaw; no prior lesson is required |
| Deliberately multi-solution or no-solution | Analyze it on that basis |
| Missing or incoherent condition/question | Ask about one spot and wait; leave the decision to the student rather than silently rewriting |

## Answering

Solve internally first and run the internal check named in the current provider's student-problem supplement: at least the unknown, units, divisibility, and remainders; for multiple solutions, the range.

- **Ordinary learning**: give the verified correct result and one checkable relationship, then invite grading; no long solution or understanding test. Never submit a deliberately wrong result as the AI's own answer, including on confirmed skills. This is requested answer material in an author-led activity, not a learner asking for a teacher-set problem's full solution; no informed-full-solution warning is needed.
- **Feynman**: follow `learn-core`'s `references/feynman-mode.md` and `references/feynman-skill-whitelist.md`. Any wrong claim is one clearly labeled hypothetical confusion, not an asserted correct result. Before the current reply ends, correct both the conclusion and error point, then invite explanation. Confirmed-skill protection and mistake eligibility stay with the whitelist; do not manufacture consecutive errors to maintain a ratio.
- **Separate contrast activity**: when useful, suggest that the session offer a separate contrast activity. After the student agrees, present a verified, traceable sample as a clearly labeled claim to check, explicitly hypothetical, not the AI's correct answer. Clarify the correct relation when closing.

## Role Boundary and Evidence

**The problem-maker leads**: the student makes the problem and grades it; the AI is the answerer. Inviting grading in ordinary learning does not switch to Feynman mode.

### Receive grading

| Student says | Action |
|---|---|
| "Right" / "same as mine" | Wrap up; use any voluntarily offered explanation or other solution directly |
| "Wrong", no location | Ask one question about your own answer, such as "Which step did I get wrong?"; if still unspecified, keep the disagreement and hand the flow choice back |
| A specific disagreement | Recheck your own solution against the conditions first; admit and fix a real error. If the student's reasoning clearly contradicts the conditions, ask one real question as the answerer; if unresolved, keep it unknown and hand the flow choice back |
| Asks for help | Confirm by a flow option whether to return to joint solving or the teaching assistant's guidance; the mainline then arranges scaffolding, practice, or diagnosis |

### Exit and recovery

The default exit after grading is wrap-up: wrap up directly without an acceptance quiz. Missing evidence is not a reason for further questioning. Apply `learn-core`'s `references/guidance-non-response-handling.md` only after the student chooses to return to teaching guidance, not to probe, shrink the problem, or request board work for evidence here.

Legacy recovery only: clarify an unresolved deliberately wrong AI answer's correct conclusion and error point before receiving the current reply or exiting; never ask why it was not noticed. New ordinary turns do not create this state. When resuming an old acceptance-style follow-up, receive the current reply under this boundary rather than continue the acceptance chain.

### Attribute evidence

Separate composition, grading/self-report, explanation, independent solving, and transfer. A valid problem, bare answer report, or affirming grade alone establishes neither solving nor transfer; independent verification is not presumed. Missing evidence remains unknown, not an error, expression difficulty, scaffold need, or reason to lower the level.

Use a justified solution volunteered before answer exposure as solving evidence. Record hints and exposure with later work: explaining a revealed answer is not independent error detection. Transfer needs demonstrated use in a meaningfully changed context or condition. AI/sample errors and failure to spot them are not student solving errors or difficulties; record actual erroneous student reasoning separately.
