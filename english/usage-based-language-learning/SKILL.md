# Usage-Based Language Learning
## Principle
Repeated meaningful encounters with form-meaning patterns support learning of constructions. Variation and use matter; rote repetition alone is insufficient.
## Variables
age, level, target_construction, known_vocabulary, interests, examples_count, context_variation, production_readiness, modalities.
## Procedure
1. Choose one functional pattern: “I want __”, “Can you __?”, “It's under __”.
2. Give several natural examples with familiar, interesting referents.
3. Vary one slot while keeping the communicative function clear.
4. Invite child-created examples linked to their own intentions.
5. Revisit the construction in a distinct context later; look for independent transfer.
6. Expand complexity gradually, without premature grammar terminology.
## Avoid
Mechanical mass drilling, teaching many patterns at once, treating memorized imitation as transferable competence.
## Example
“I want a banana.” “I want a red kite.” “What do you want for our picnic?”
