# Faithful Reading

## Whole page, then lines

Examine the whole image first: question boundaries, numbering, arrows, crossed-out lines, side notes, and whether each mark is printed, the student's ink, or an adult mark. Then read line by line in the order written. Do not invent omitted steps or merge lines into an idealized solution.

## What to transcribe

Copy exactly: digits, signs, decimal points, parentheses, fraction-bar extent, exponents, roots, equality and inequality signs, units, column alignment, carries and borrows, diagram labels, and dashed versus solid auxiliary lines. Keep the original order, crossed-out attempts, and corrections.

LaTeX is a readable rendering, never a corrected mathematical equivalent. Preserve an invalid equation as written instead of normalizing it.

## Ambiguities

- Mark the region and plausible alternatives, for example `line 2, last digit: 1 or 7`; use `unreadable` when necessary.
- Never assign invented numeric confidence scores; a visually clear token may still be wrong.
- Do not equate a plausible expression with legibility.
- If an ambiguity affects the coming judgment, return it as the one pending question; if it does not, continue and keep the token unconfirmed.
- Do not silently resolve ambiguity from context, the printed question, or the expected answer.

## Printed, ink, and adult marks

Keep the printed question, the student's handwritten work, and adult or teacher marks separate. A printed page is material, not the student's performance. A crossed-out attempt is written history, not the current answer.

## Subject hints

The current subject contract may supply notation hints, for example how a fraction bar, a column, an exponent, or a diagram mark is drawn. Apply those hints when reading. With none, use the generic rules above and do not invent subject rules.

## Limits of a static image

A photo documents written steps, not timing, intention, erased thought, or the student's mental process. Describe a suspected misconception as a hypothesis at most, and never as an established diagnosis. Do not infer sequence from layout, and do not read unrelated page content beyond what the task needs.

The transcription is a reading index, not a reproduction. Do not promise recognition accuracy; a clear-looking token can still be wrong, and the original image remains the only faithful artifact.

## Several problems on one page

Transcribe every visible problem and label the boundaries; do not choose the problem the student will work on. The current teaching owner selects one, or asks one question when the selection changes the next action.
