# Guided Inquiry and Mathematical Proof

1. Give a constrained pattern or geometric situation and ask what the child notices.
2. Invite a precise conjecture: “for which numbers/figures does this hold?”
3. Test varied cases, including boundary cases, and actively search for a counterexample.
4. Distinguish “worked in five examples” from a general reason; build an argument using a diagram, parity, grouping or algebra-ready sentence.
5. Ask the child to state any conditions and revise the conjecture.

Example: First `n` odd numbers appear to sum to `n²`. Build successive square borders of `2n−1` dots; explain why every step preserves the rule. Do not describe induction as established by observation alone.

Use guided prompts and targeted examples. If the learner has no foothold, teach prerequisites explicitly. Avoid presenting formal proof terminology as a barrier; do not claim empirical checking proves a universal statement.

Background: Mathematical reasoning/proof in [NCTM-aligned mathematical practices](https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/mathpractices.asp).
