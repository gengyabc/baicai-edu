# Counterexamples and Proposition Direction

Read for testing a universal/conditional claim, a proposed counterexample or a necessary condition, including supplied examples. `math-high-session` keeps the act. Use `references/proof-teaching.md` for scope/direction and `references/derivation-teaching.md` for bridges.

## Validate the two ends

For P → Q, identify the stated objects and all premises. A counterexample satisfies **every premise** and falsifies Q. Do not drop a premise or infer P from Q. Clarify one decisive uncertainty; test the first missing end using a smaller comparable case or probe if useful. For an existence conclusion, one failed construction is not enough: rule out all allowed constructions for that object.

One valid counterexample refutes the universal, not every instance. A working instance neither proves the universal nor rescues it. Preserve a correct necessary-condition argument: failure of the condition rules the property out; passing it alone does not establish the converse. Seeking a converse takes a proof over the stated range or a counterexample. Failed search leaves it open; a justified finite exhaustive check can prove it.

Keep the next inference open: check a near-miss premise, offer a nearby case after stalled search, or inspect a working instance after overgeneralization. Teacher-supplied cases follow **Guide before revealing** and the Informed full-solution choice in `learn-core`; label hints/cases separately from student checks. Reproducing a supplied case is supported understanding, not independent construction. Use `references/prerequisite-learning.md` for confirmed tool gaps.

## Private counterexample checks

Keep answers/routes private in independent work. Informed full-solution choice governs confirmed disclosure.

| Refutation | Verify |
|---|---|
| Balance does not guarantee covering | Verify balance and show every legal covering fails (e.g. an isolated required part) |
| Local degree does not ensure edge-once traversal | Verify the degrees and separate global obstruction; retain any connectivity premise |
| Connectivity does not ensure edge-once traversal | Verify connectivity and too many odd-degree vertices for entry/exit pairing |
| New premise strengthens the claim | Recheck it; do not reuse an object now outside scope |
| Necessity mistaken for sufficiency | Preserve its valid necessity argument; a successful case can limit overgeneralization |

A general sufficiency theorem needs its applicable conditions and usable justification.
