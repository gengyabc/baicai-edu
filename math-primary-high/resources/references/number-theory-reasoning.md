Negotiate a proof of unique prime factorization separately if requested; factor examples use the theorem, not prove it.

## Private verification

Keep answers/routes private in independent work. Use `learn-core`'s informed full-solution choice (wait and same-task confirmation; no prerequisite or compulsory exercise).

- **Factor pairs:** Every divisor has one partner. Order by the smaller factor, justify stopping when it would exceed its partner (multiplication comparisons suffice), check divisibility and count an equal pair once.
- **Prime choices:** a divisor picks copies of each prime; each valid choice gives one divisor, every divisor is covered, and distinct choices give distinct divisors. Explain correspondence before multiplying choice counts.
- **Complete a power:** equal pairs/triples of each prime are necessary for a square/cube. Verify the actual product; 1 is allowed when already a power. For least, show every completion contains the missing factors (extra primes cannot replace them), or that every smaller allowed factor is exhaustively excluded. Accept a justified finite check. Recheck domain, structure and minimality when the number or power changes.
- **Parity:** two odd leftovers form a pair; three leave one. Or represent each odd number as 2a+1, with a ranging over the relevant integers/nonnegative integers, and justify coverage. Samples do not prove the sum rule; products need their own reason.
- **Prime scope:** primes are integers greater than 1 with exactly two positive divisors. 2 alone is even: each larger even integer has the extra divisor 2. This refutes “all primes are odd”; 1 is not prime.

Completion is a nonduplicating, complete count, a reason excluding smaller completions, or a general argument over the claimed scope. Distinguish bare results and teacher-supplied groupings from independent route selection.
