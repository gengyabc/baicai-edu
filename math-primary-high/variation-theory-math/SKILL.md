# Variation Theory for Math

Pick one critical feature of a concept. Present a **small sequence** (2–4) of examples with one meaningful variation at a time; have the child name what changed, what stayed invariant, and why the conclusion changes or persists.

- Contrast examples and non-examples to make necessary conditions visible.
- Then vary irrelevant surface detail while preserving structure to prompt generalization.
- Ask for a child-generated example and counterexample.

Example: Compare `1/2 + 1/3`, `1/2 + 1/6`, and `1/2 × 1/3`. Ask what the operation demands from denominator units, rather than supplying three unrelated procedures. Check transfer with different numerators.

Avoid changing multiple critical features at once, tricks before conceptual grounding, or excessive near-identical drills. If prerequisite knowledge is absent, route to explicit instruction or CPA first.

Background: Variation theory associated with Ference Marton; [EEF guidance on building connected mathematical knowledge](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/maths-ks-2-3/).
