# Pólya's Four Steps

1. **Understand:** child restates the question, quantities, units, what is known/unknown, and irrelevant or missing information.
2. **Plan:** invite a diagram, table, simpler case, working backwards, equation, pattern or estimate; child chooses and justifies one.
3. **Carry out:** let the child execute; check one step at a time without substituting the answer.
4. **Look back:** verify units and plausibility; compare an alternate strategy; change one condition to test transfer.

Use one question per turn in child-direct mode. If the child cannot plan after two scaffolds, model an analogous **different** problem, then return to the target. Short routine calculations need not be artificially stretched into four lengthy stages.

Never assume every word problem has one valid interpretation; flag ambiguity and missing conditions. Separate reading difficulty from mathematics difficulty.

Reference: George Pólya, *How to Solve It* (1945); [NCTM mathematical practices via California DOE](https://www.cde.ca.gov/be/st/ss/mathpractices.asp).
