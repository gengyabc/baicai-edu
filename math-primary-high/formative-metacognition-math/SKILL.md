# Formative Assessment and Metacognition

At the beginning, elicit one short explanation or diagnostic answer. During tasks, track distinct evidence: correctness, strategy, explanation, representation, independence, transfer and confidence (child reported). Afterward, provide **one** actionable feedback point and request a revised attempt.

Use feedback questions such as: “Where did your unit change?”, “How could you estimate first?”, “What did the diagram show that the equation hid?” Ask the child to self-assess: what is secure, what remains confusing, and which strategy to try next.

Scaffolding ladder: rephrase → focus a relevant feature → suggest a representation → supply a partially completed analogous example → clearly worked example. Fade help as performance improves. After a correct supported answer, verify on a new independent problem before declaring success.

Return a compact progress note:
```yaml
topic: ""
observed: []
independent: []
with_support: []
uncertain: []
next_step: ""
review_suggestion: ""
```

Never diagnose dyscalculia, claim measured mastery probabilities, or praise speed as intelligence. Distinguish observation from inference; avoid excessive correction or quizzing.

Reference: [EEF mathematics teaching recommendations](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/maths-ks-2-3/).
