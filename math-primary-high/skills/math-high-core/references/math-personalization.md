# Math Personalization

Shared boundaries (must not override the goal, privacy, one detail per turn, or self-assessment priority) follow `learn-core`'s `references/prestart-settings-interpretation.md`. This file specifies how that information is used in upper-primary math.

Interests, preferences, fatigue, and the student's own choice of activity decide what the next activity looks like; they never decide the mathematics under discussion, the strategy stage, or the difficulty level.

## Interest Bridging

`personal.interests` does not decide the subject matter or force a story format; task formats follow `references/problem-design.md`.

A bridge requires all of: the problem has already been given; the student has tried or explicitly said they cannot do it; and there is no way in sight to keep moving forward. The bridge uses a single interest detail to restate the same problem's relationships, knowns, and unknowns, then returns to the relationship itself; at most once per problem. Once the student can continue, end the interest restatement; the next problem is chosen for the purpose in `references/problem-design.md`, with interests still used only for bridging.

Interests may also select a real situation for the application-modeling act (a budget, a journey, a recipe, a survey) when the student explicitly wants a real-life task; the situation supplies the setting, not the numbers or the answer.

`personal.most_exciting_event` stays out of problems; when no usable interest exists, it may serve as that one detail. Upsetting experiences and privacy follow prestart.

## Preferences

Subject `profile.preferences` only change presentation:

- `likes_step_by_step`: when working a multi-step problem, present the plan as clearly separated steps; do not pre-compute them for the student.
- `likes_examples`: when a concept is opaque, start from a worked or half-worked example, then return to the student's problem.
- `likes_real_world`: prefer a real situation for the application-modeling act; still ask the student to state assumptions.

## Fatigue, Choice, and Pacing

- When the student reports fatigue, frustration, or reluctance, first check whether they want to stop, choose a different activity, or hear a full explanation; do not keep pushing practice because a plan was made.
- When the student chooses an activity themselves, honour their choice inside the current relationship when it is feasible; do not replace it with "the next thing the plan says".
- A pace that works for one student is not a speed target; never record speed as a stage or ability signal.
