# Rule: Guidance Non-Response Handling

First judge per `references/confirm-before-accept.md` whether more evidence is still needed; run this flow only when there is a real gap. A short answer can be a complete answer; when the reason is unclear, leave it unknown and do not mark it as a weak area by word count or grade. Subject-specific comparisons are supplied by each subject skill.

## 1. Open probe

Build on what the student has already said and ask just one open follow-up, then wait (for example, when they only give a conclusion, ask "How did you come up with that?").

- Keep the student's own method; do not list candidate approaches or demonstrate first.
- When there is already a checkable approach, go straight to confirmation without re-probing.
- An empty message only means no content was received; keep the pending question and do not increase the difficulty or confirmation-failure counts. An unanswered question does not count as a failed probe, and no scaffolding is added in the same reply.

## 2. Route by response

| Observed response | Next action |
|---|---|
| Brief but explains the approach, or shows an effective process, evidence, or operation | Continue confirming from the actual evidence; expression length does not affect the judgment |
| Still short, silent, or not used to or not wanting to say more | Lower the expression burden and wait; when the reason is unclear, leave it unknown |
| "I don't know how to say it" | Invite them to write, draw, or show the process they already have, first distinguishing expression difficulty from knowledge difficulty |
| Explicitly says they can't do it, asks for help, or shows a specific error, contradiction, or sticking point | Give the minimal scaffold around that sticking point (see next section) |
| Asks to skip, change the question, or end | Handle per `references/session-end-signals.md`; a skip is not itself evidence of ability |

A valid but unexpected method, or just saying "got it," is not enough to conclude they can't do it; when evidence is needed, have the student show one actual step. For different-method and confirmation details, `references/confirm-before-accept.md` governs.

## 3. Minimal scaffolding and backing off

Give only one hint per turn that is just enough to continue, leaving the part for the student to complete; first narrow down to the step where they are stuck, and give material, a partial demonstration, or a half-finished step only when truly needed. Grade only adjusts language and material.

- Wait after each hint before deciding whether to add a layer; do not list several methods at once or complete the reasoning for the student.
- The follow-up budget follows the confirmation protocol. If there is still no evidence after lowering the burden, keep "strategy to be confirmed," move to a neighboring task or confirm the next step per end signals; do not require a spoken explanation first.
- At most 3 scaffolds; if still stuck, hand to subject diagnosis, passing observations and unknowns separately.
- The log records observable facts (such as "gave only a conclusion, strategy to be confirmed"); difficulties, errors, and stage adjustments need task evidence, and the teaching assistant's own thinking does not count as independent student evidence.
